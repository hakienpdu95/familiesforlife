Why the F.A.C.T.S. model is the key to search everywhere optimization
Learn how to boost visibility across search, social, reputation, and AI using SOCi's F.A.C.T.S. model for multi-location brands.
<div id="articleContent"> <div class="bialty-container"> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">For years, Google has <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content" target="_blank" rel="noopener sponsored nofollow">used the E-E-A-T framework</a> to assess the quality of web content. The acronym stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Google recommends that content creators follow E-E-A-T principles because they signal value to searchers and align with what Google’s search algorithms are designed to reward.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Another long-standing Google framework is Relevance, Distance, and Prominence. Google identifies these three signals in its document, <a href="https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener sponsored nofollow">Tips to Improve Your Local Ranking on Google</a>, as the primary factors that influence local search rankings.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Frameworks like these can help you identify what to improve to boost search rankings. But until now, there hasn’t been a comparable framework for AI visibility or one that reflects the priorities of multi-location marketers across search, social, reputation, and AI — the integrated approach we’ve come to call search everywhere optimization.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">That’s why we developed the <a href="https://sociai-www.nyc3.digitaloceanspaces.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/17133748/FACTS-Checklist-PDF_June23-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener sponsored nofollow">F.A.C.T.S. model</a>. F.A.C.T.S. stands for the five factors we believe matter most in a holistic strategy: Freshness, Authority, Consistency, Trust, and Semantic Relevance.</p> <div class="wp-block-image"> <figure class="wp-block-heading">Freshness</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Freshness refers to how recently you publish content on your website and third-party profiles like Google, Yelp, and Facebook. Fresh content matters to people, and search engines and AI platforms also strongly favor it.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The average URL cited by AI platforms is 25.7% newer than those cited in traditional search, a recent Ahrefs study <a href="https://ziptie.dev/blog/content-refresh-strategy-for-ai-citations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener sponsored nofollow">found</a>. More than 70% of AI-cited pages were updated within the past 12 months, according to AirOps; meanwhile, 76.4% of ChatGPT’s top-cited pages were updated within the past 30 days, SE Ranking found.</p> <h2 id="h-authority" class="wp-block-heading">Authority</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">On this point, our model is similar to Google’s. Authority reflects how a brand demonstrates leadership in its industry.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">For example, if you’ve been in business since 1963, listing that in your Google profile signals authority. Trusted sources can also reinforce it, including professional certifications, best-of lists, and positive coverage in online publications.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Because authority is a complex signal, definitive research on its importance can be hard to find. Its inclusion in E-E-A-T underscores its significance, and Google’s Prominence factor in local search describes a similar concept.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Brands publishing </div> </div> <h2 id="h-consistency" class="wp-block-heading">Consistency</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In local visibility, consistency has evolved. It once meant listing each location’s name, address, phone number, and website uniformly across as many directories as possible. As the directory landscape contracted and Google grew dominant, these long-tail citations became largely irrelevant.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">But AI’s need to ground answers in trusted sources has renewed the importance of managing your brand’s presence across multiple platforms — not hundreds of directories, but the handful of sources AI platforms are most likely to cite for local queries.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Google Maps, business websites, Yelp, and Facebook (in that order) are the sources most often cited for local queries on ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity, <a href="https://www.soci.ai/ai-visibility-report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener sponsored nofollow">SOCi’s research</a> indicates. However, top sources vary by industry, and individual brands may have citation profiles that differ from the norm.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Our <a href="https://www.soci.ai/lvi-2026/" target="_blank" rel="noopener sponsored nofollow">Local Visibility Index</a> shows that inconsistency is a key driver of inaccurate AI mentions and weak brand visibility. While 98% of the brand locations studied had claimed Google profiles, only 80% had claimed Yelp profiles, and just 53% managed Facebook store pages. signals from outside the brand that reflect approval from both consumers and experts. In local search, trust is conveyed largely through ratings and reviews on platforms like Google and Yelp, which AI platforms use as a primary factor when recommending local brands.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Our <a href="https://www.soci.ai/insights/consumer-behavior-index/" target="_blank" rel="noopener sponsored nofollow">Consumer Behavior Index</a> shows that 92% of consumers consult online reviews when choosing a local business. The average traditional search query is four words, while the average AI query is 23, according to <a href="https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/traditional-search-vs-ai-search/" target="_blank" rel="noopener sponsored nofollow">Orbit Media</a>. Consumers using AI ask longer, more nuanced questions, so brands must create detailed, useful content to avoid exclusion.</p> <h2 id="h-f-a-c-t-s-is-a-filter-through-which-all-optimization-opportunities-should-be-evaluated" class="wp-block-heading">F.A.C.T.S. is a filter through which all optimization opportunities should be evaluated</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most marketing teams don’t have time to chase every algorithm rumor.
